Photographic Education

I have always somehow throughout my life wonder about the knowledge I never once used aside from the textbooks. Yet those scary formulas, those complex biological taxonomic words: those are the exact stuff, a high school student shall have to survive. As someone currently entering into the practical world, it seems like so much was insignificant to reach where I am now. If this whole process would have been a business venture or start-up, it would have been a flop, a bad joke tbh.

The question is: so what is the significance of learning frog's biological taxonomy, or remembering the periodic table or Physics's units which my school or even university does not have instruments to measure let alone standard environment. What was the purpose of those pretentious books that told us all along only one thing: the world is a fair & square game? What was the purpose of schools if at the end, the kid ended up hating education despite spending his/her whole childhood there? What was the purpose of those moral essays, who preached that world is either black or white? I personally think in 1-of-n problems is the way we perceive knowledge. I will quote Sylvia Plath here:

“What I fear most, I think, is the death of the imagination. When the sky outside is merely pink, and the rooftops merely black: that photographic mind which paradoxically tells the truth, but the worthless truth, about the world.”

So, knowledge suffers the same dilemma. What shall I do with the fact that frog is “Rana Tigrina” that the Periodic Table has 7 rows, and Physics's unit of meter is smaller then kilo-meter? The human mind has a special factor that extends its curiosity towards things: Experience. We invest in thing which we can experience. Rather then like a photographic knowledge that needs to be slapped on the brain, brain must experience the knowledge just like it experience the human love, nature's peace, God's fear. For instance, we never say to child 2 + 2 = 4. We give him/her the trick of how to experience, and that experience is "+" sign. The very first principle in every system is an anchor for experience. Indeed, some form of knowledge is dissected from the top-down approach, and this can be an hindrance, but in that case, we must go for hybrid methodologies. Since our modernization has led us to believe that so far each and every knowledge is highly overlapped with each other, the first principle of one study discipline can be an anchor for the discipline which is top-down centric. The best example is the anchor of computer science, engineering in biology. The question should not be how many mitochondria are in the cell, but rather why are they, changing them do what, do we can replace them whilst maintaining their products constant? Each concept in knowledge is a movie, with characters contrary to photographic/factual representations we’re used to.
